# Choosing AI meeting notes tools: recording, summaries and privacy

[简体中文](meeting-notes.md) · [Directory](../../README.en.md) · [Privacy and selection](../privacy-and-selection.md)

First establish whether you need to handle live meetings or existing material, then check transcription, summaries, sharing and deletion. These notes summarize public provider sources; they are **not hands-on testing or privacy certification**. Language, plan, platform and feature availability depend on the version. Checked: 2026-10-29.

## Start with your meeting workflow

- [Otter.ai](https://otter.ai/): its Notetaker can join Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams to record, transcribe and summarize. Its [official guide](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/4425393298327-Otter-Notetaker-Overview) also describes calendar connections and automatic joining settings.
- [Fathom](https://www.fathom.ai/): its official help describes recording and transcribing live meetings to generate summaries. The [help page](https://help.fathom.video/en/articles/6049729) says external recording uploads are currently unsupported; check the latest support.
- [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/): its website describes meeting notes, action items and questions about meeting context, and says it can use computer audio. Check your device, meeting software and settings.

See more candidates in the [meetings and collaboration directory](../../README.en.md#meetings-and-collaboration). Test Chinese recognition, overlapping speakers and specialist terms under comparable conditions.

## Check privacy before comparing

1. Before recording or transcribing, check applicable consent requirements and organizational rules, notify participants as required, and review automatic joining or recording settings.
2. Establish who processes audio, transcripts and summaries, how long they are kept, who can view or share them, and how to export, delete and revoke calendar access.
3. Begin with fictional meeting material; if a tool only records live meetings, use a simulated meeting with participant consent. Check names, numbers, decisions, action items and owners.
4. Review and remove sensitive information before sharing. Do not include real meeting contents, account links or personal data in public issues, PRs or commit history.
